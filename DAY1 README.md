# Data Analytics – Daily Task Update
## Module 1: Introduction to Data Analytics

### 1. What Is Data?
Data refers to raw facts, figures, observations, or records collected from different sources. Data may include numbers, text, dates, images, or other forms of information. By processing and analyzing data, we can identify meaningful patterns and support decision-making.

**Example:** Sales amounts, customer names, product prices, employee attendance records, and customer feedback.

### 2. What Is Data Analytics?
Data Analytics is the process of collecting, cleaning, transforming, and analyzing data to identify patterns, trends, relationships, and useful insights. It helps organizations make informed, data-driven decisions and improve business performance.

**Example:** Analyzing monthly sales data to identify the highest-selling products and understand why sales increased or decreased.

### 3. Role of a Data Analyst
A Data Analyst converts raw data into meaningful information that supports business decisions. The role involves working with spreadsheets, databases, analytical tools, and visualization platforms.

**Key responsibilities:**
- **Data Collection:** Gather data from databases, Excel files, APIs, and other sources.
- **Data Cleaning:** Identify and handle missing values, duplicates, inconsistent formats, and errors.
- **Data Analysis:** Examine data to discover trends, patterns, and relationships.
- **Data Visualization:** Present findings through charts, graphs, and dashboards.
- **Reporting:** Prepare reports that communicate important metrics and findings.
- **Business Insights:** Recommend actions based on analytical results.

**Example:** A Data Analyst examines sales performance and recommends strategies to improve revenue.

### 4. Data Analyst vs. Data Scientist vs. Data Engineer

**Data Analyst:** Analyzes historical and current data to understand business performance, answer questions, and support decisions. Common tools include Excel, SQL, Power BI, and Tableau.

**Data Scientist:** Uses statistics, programming, and machine learning to develop predictive models and identify complex patterns. Common tools include Python, R, and machine learning libraries.

**Data Engineer:** Builds and maintains data pipelines, databases, and infrastructure to ensure data is collected, processed, stored, and made available reliably. Common tools include SQL, Python, cloud platforms, and data-processing frameworks.

**In simple terms:**
- Data Analyst: What happened, and why?
- Data Scientist: What might happen next?
- Data Engineer: How can data be collected, processed, and delivered reliably?

*Note: These responsibilities can overlap depending on the organization.*

### 5. Types of Data
Data can be classified according to its nature and format.

**A. Qualitative Data:** Descriptive, non-numerical information that represents categories or characteristics.

Examples: Customer feedback, product category, employee department, and payment method.

**B. Quantitative Data:** Numerical information that can be counted or measured.

Examples: Sales revenue, customer age, product quantity, and monthly expenses.

**C. Structured Data:** Data organized according to a predefined format, usually rows and columns.

Examples: SQL database tables, Excel spreadsheets, and sales transaction records.

**D. Semi-Structured Data:** Data that does not follow a rigid tabular structure but contains tags, keys, or other organizational elements.

Examples: JSON files, XML documents, and many application log formats.

**E. Unstructured Data:** Data that does not follow a predefined tabular schema and often requires specialized techniques to process.

Examples: Images, videos, audio recordings, emails, and free-text documents.

### 6. Structured vs. Unstructured Data

**Structured Data**
- Organized in a predefined format.
- Commonly stored in rows and columns.
- Easier to query using SQL when stored in a relational database.
- Suitable for traditional reporting and dashboard creation.

**Example:** A sales table containing Order ID, Customer Name, Product, Date, and Revenue.

**Unstructured Data**
- Does not follow a predefined tabular schema.
- May contain text, audio, images, or video.
- Often requires text processing, image analysis, or other specialized techniques.
- Can provide insights that are not available in traditional tables.

**Example:** Customer reviews, recorded support calls, and product images.

### 7. Qualitative vs. Quantitative Data

**Qualitative Data**
- Describes characteristics, categories, or qualities.
- Usually represented by labels or descriptive values.
- Can be classified into nominal and ordinal data.

Examples:
- Nominal: Product category, city, payment method.
- Ordinal: Low, medium, high satisfaction.

**Quantitative Data**
- Represents quantities that can be counted or measured.
- Supports mathematical calculations and statistical analysis.
- Can be classified into discrete and continuous data.

Examples:
- Discrete: Number of orders, number of employees.
- Continuous: Product weight, delivery time, temperature.

**Key difference:** Qualitative data explains *what kind or category*, while quantitative data explains *how many or how much*.

### 8. Common Data Sources
A Data Analyst collects information from multiple sources depending on the business problem.

- **Databases:** Store organized records that can be queried using SQL. Examples: MySQL, PostgreSQL, and SQL Server.
- **Excel Files:** Store tabular data for calculations, analysis, and reporting.
- **CSV Files:** Store tabular information in a plain-text, comma-separated format.
- **APIs:** Allow applications to exchange data programmatically.
- **Websites:** Provide publicly available information that may be collected through permitted web scraping.
- **Surveys and Forms:** Collect customer opinions, satisfaction ratings, and demographic information.
- **Business Applications:** Provide information from CRM, ERP, HR, finance, and sales systems.
- **Logs and Sensors:** Record application activity, system events, and measurements from connected devices.

**Example:** A sales analyst may combine transaction data from SQL Server with monthly targets from Excel to evaluate sales performance.

### 9. Data Analyst Workflow
The Data Analytics workflow is a systematic process that converts raw data into actionable business insights.

**Step 1 — Define the Business Problem:** Understand the objective, stakeholder requirements, and questions the analysis must answer.

**Step 2 — Collect Data:** Obtain relevant data from databases, spreadsheets, APIs, surveys, or other sources.

**Step 3 — Clean Data:** Handle missing values, duplicate records, incorrect entries, inconsistent formats, and other quality issues.

**Step 4 — Transform and Prepare Data:** Standardize formats, combine datasets, create calculated columns, and organize data for analysis.

**Step 5 — Analyze Data:** Use SQL queries, Excel formulas, pivot tables, and statistical techniques to identify patterns and trends.

**Step 6 — Visualize Data:** Present the results using appropriate charts, graphs, KPI cards, and dashboards.

**Step 7 — Generate Insights:** Explain what the results mean, identify possible causes, and connect findings to business objectives.

**Step 8 — Communicate and Support Decisions:** Share findings through reports or presentations and recommend practical actions.

**Step 9 — Monitor Results:** Track KPIs and review updated data to determine whether the recommended actions are effective.

**Workflow Summary:**

Business Problem → Data Collection → Data Cleaning → Data Transformation → Data Analysis → Visualization → Insights → Business Decisions → Monitoring

### 10. Key Takeaways
- Data consists of raw facts that can be processed into useful information.
- Data Analytics transforms data into insights that support decision-making.
- A Data Analyst collects, cleans, analyzes, visualizes, and communicates data.
- Data can be classified by its nature, such as qualitative or quantitative, and by its format, such as structured or unstructured.
- Common data sources include databases, spreadsheets, APIs, surveys, and business applications.
- A successful analysis begins with a clear business problem and ends with actionable insights.
- Data quality, appropriate analysis, and clear communication are essential for reliable results.

**Practical Objective:** Understand the fundamentals of data and the Data Analytics workflow before moving on to Excel, SQL, data cleaning, statistics, and dashboard development.
