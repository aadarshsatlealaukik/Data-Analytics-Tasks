Day 11 tasks summary 


1. Distribution
Distribution describes how data values are spread, including their frequency, center, and variability.
Business example: Understanding how customer salaries are distributed.

2. Normal Distribution
A bell-shaped, symmetric distribution where the mean, median, and mode are equal.
68–95–99.7 rule: Approximately 68% of values fall within 1 standard deviation, 95% within 2, and 99.7% within 3 for a normal distribution.

3. Skewness
Skewness measures the asymmetry of a distribution.
- Zero: Approximately symmetric.
- Positive (right-skewed): Long tail toward higher values.
- Negative (left-skewed): Long tail toward lower values.

4. Outliers
Outliers are observations unusually far from most other values.
Example: Most orders are worth ₹500–₹2,000, but one order is worth ₹50,000.

5. IQR (Interquartile Range)
IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of data and helps identify potential outliers.
Formula: \(IQR=Q_3-Q_1\)

6. Correlation
Correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables.
Its coefficient \(r\) ranges from \(-1\) to \(+1\).

7. Covariance
Covariance indicates whether two variables tend to move in the same direction or opposite directions.
- Positive: They tend to move together.
- Negative: They tend to move oppositely.
- Near zero: Little linear co-movement, though nonlinear relationships may exist.

8. Scatter Plot
A chart that uses dots to show the relationship between two numerical variables.
Example: Advertising spend on the X-axis and sales revenue on the Y-axis.




9. Business Interpretation
Business interpretation means converting statistical results into useful decisions, such as identifying unusual transactions, understanding sales patterns, or evaluating the relationship between marketing spending and revenue.
